FuturAir Airport Design Proposal

A multidisciplinary Overseas Immersion Programme project proposing a next-generation international airport hub in Central Scotland, developed across civil engineering, mechanical engineering, and computer science perspectives.

OIP note: a key part of this project was learning how to work across disciplines rather than only within one technical area. The proposal had to stay coherent across civil, mechanical, and computer science decisions, so collaboration and handoff thinking were just as important as the individual design ideas.

This project was completed as part of the SIT-UofG Overseas Immersion Programme and centered on designing a new international airport concept for Central Scotland. Our group proposed FuturAir, also framed in the poster as Pentland Hills International Airport, with the design anchored around three principles: a seamless journey, a sustainable landmark, and a future-proof hub.

Because the team combined students from different courses, the project went beyond a standard computing assignment. The final proposal had to align airport layout decisions, environmental strategy, structural thinking, operational flow, and digital systems into one coherent concept rather than treating each stream as a separate mini-project.

I was part of the computer science stream, where the focus was on the airport's digital and operational technology layer. Based on the submitted report and poster, that included security camera planning around immigration, storage and redundancy thinking, and innovation ideas such as 3D virtual wayfinding and passenger-flow analysis supported by the camera infrastructure.

Project brief

  • Developed a next-generation airport concept for Scotland that had to balance passenger experience, sustainability, and future-readiness.
  • Framed the proposal around three core principles: seamless journey, sustainable landmark, and future-proof hub.
  • Produced a combined design outcome that brought research, infrastructure thinking, and digital systems ideas into one airport concept.

Computer science aspect

  • Focused on immigration as a critical facility and planned camera placement across queue zones, counters, e-gates, corridors, backrooms, and screening areas.
  • Considered the operational side of the surveillance system through storage sizing, onsite retention, cloud backup, and redundancy planning.
  • Extended the concept beyond security alone by proposing 3D virtual wayfinding and passenger-flow analysis built on the same camera infrastructure.

My contribution

  • Worked within the computer science stream of the group project, contributing to the digital-airport concept layer of the overall proposal.
  • Helped shape how the airport could use cameras and supporting architecture not just for monitoring, but also for navigation and operational awareness.
  • Learned how to present technical ideas in a way that could align with civil and mechanical decisions inside one shared multidisciplinary deliverable.

Why it mattered

  • The project was a good exercise in working with people from other courses, where your own work had to fit the constraints and language of a broader engineering proposal.
  • It pushed the computer science portion beyond pure software features by framing it as airport operations, infrastructure planning, and user movement through space.
  • It also highlighted how digital systems can contribute to both security and passenger experience when they are designed as part of the wider built environment.
